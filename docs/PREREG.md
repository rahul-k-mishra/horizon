## 1. Claim and non-claims

> For frame-only, prior-free agents that act on built-in assumptions, on the levels of unseen deterministic grid games, among levels whose games match the agents' assumptions equally (the same number of the assumptions A1 to A5 holds), a pre-run measure of the level's search, the fewest actions to a win found by breadth-first search within a fixed number of states, ranks the levels by first-level completion above chance.

**[decided 14 Nov 2026, decisions 1 to 3]** This replaces the claim of v0, which read: "a pre-run measure of the size of the level's search (the number of reachable states) ranks the levels by first-level completion better than the match between the game's mechanics and the agents' assumptions (the number of the assumptions A1 to A5 that hold)."

- H2, search size, is the hypothesis; H1, mechanics match, is held fixed by the comparison: only levels of games with the same mechanics score are compared, so the mechanics score cannot rank them. The registered contrast of v0, Delta = AUROC_H - AUROC_M, becomes a registered secondary estimate (Section 7.7), reported with its smallest detectable difference (Section 7.8).
- *Reason:* stage D showed that the contrast of v0 has power 0.0517 (estimate 1) and 0.0345 (estimate 2) for N at the development difference on 26 games, and needs 475 to 887 game clusters for power 0.80 for N (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 1). The number of games is a property of the suite, and no threshold or agent change alters it. The test above is the part of "search over mechanics" that 26 games can carry: for N, with the fewest actions to a win on same-mechanics pairs, power 0.7499 at the registered level if the confirmatory AUROC is near the development one (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 6; the power at the C fixed is in Section 7.8).
- Not claimed: how much better search predicts than mechanics; that a level with a longer solution is harder for any agent other than the three evidence agents; that search size causes failure; that the result transfers to games with random elements, which are excluded by construction (Section 4); that the agents are strong; that anything holds for agents with visual or language priors; any theorem.
- **A registered limit [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 8]:** on the development data N and R gave the same outcome on 1,547 of 1,560 units (99.2%), against 74.9% for N and I1 (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 4). Under the Support rule "N, and at least one of R and I1" (Section 10), R will meet the second condition almost whenever N meets the first, if the agents agree as closely on the confirmatory levels. The Support rule stands as drafted, with this known; the report gives the agreement of N and R, and of N and I1, on the confirmatory levels beside the outcome.
- **A registered limit [changed 25 Oct 2026]:** restricting the suite to deterministic games removes most of its A4 failures: A4 fails on 100 of 550 units across all 110 grid-physics games, against 5 of 150 in the 30 deterministic games (logs/truth_vgdl.log), and it holds on all 26 confirmatory games, as does A1 (logs/sdd.log). So the confirmatory suite varies little on A4, and the study tests H1 mainly through A2, A3 and A5. The smallest detectable difference (Section 7.8) must be read with that in mind; scripts/sdd.py reports the mechanics score's variation in the confirmatory suite beside its number. After the A2 decision of 25 October 2026 (Section 5), A1 and A4 still hold on all 26 confirmatory games (logs/sdd.log).
- **A4's first clause [decided 25 Oct 2026; the limit first recorded here was removed the same day by a check in the generator]:** The generator checks A4's first clause, that every move the avatar makes without effect is explained by a sprite that blocks it. An undoAll line puts every sprite back whichever sprite is named first (third_party/gvgai/src/ontology/effects/unary/UndoAll.java:40-57), so a line without the avatar first also cancels the avatar's move. Every sprite such a line names must be agent-driven or pass the same tests as a blocker; otherwise A4 fails. The agent-driven set is the largest set consistent with itself: a crate pushed along by another crate the avatar pushed counts (thecitadel), and a move whose only partner is the edge of the level, as a pushed box wrapping round, does not count against a sprite (wrapsokoban). Such lines exist in 14 of the 26 confirmatory games, every one of them now cited by the A4 clauses (logs/a4_unchecked.log). The check changes no verdict in the deterministic suite (logs/truth_diff_a4clause.log). **What remains a limit:** only stepBack and undoAll are treated as ways of cancelling a move. In the 30 deterministic games, the other effects applied to the avatar as the first sprite are transforms, kills, resource and health changes, shieldFrom and one wrapAround, and no undoAll line has a notStype option (logs/a4_unchecked.log). None of these cancels a move.

## 3. Environment contract and adapter

**Contract** (study 1's, unchanged): reset(seed) gives a grid of integer colour indices; step(action) gives grid, level_end, game_end; a fixed action list per game. No reward, no score, no symbolic state, no text.

**GVGAI adapter [registered here]:**
- Framework: GVGAI at commit e58ff6597319155218759cc178931e05b81b524c, compiled with javac from a pinned Temurin JDK, called in-process through JPype1 1.7.1 under Python 3.11.
- Grid: one cell per level cell, read from the game's observation grid. Each cell's index is given online, in order of first appearance within the run, to the set of visible sprite types in the cell; sprites with hidden=True or invisible=True are not drawn and not counted; the avatar's orientations are one index. Known limit: two sprite types drawn with the same image get different indices.
- Action list: the avatar's available actions without the null action, in GVGAI's order.
- level_end: the game reports a win. game_end: the game reports a loss, or GVGAI's tick limit ends the episode; the next episode starts on the same level.
- Seeds 1000 to 1009 seed the agent; the games are deterministic, so the seed changes nothing in the game.

## 5. Truth tables: the mechanics vector

Per (game, level), from the description and the level file, by scripts/truth_vgdl.py (synthetic tests scripts/test_truth_vgdl.py, passing with warnings as errors), output docs/truth/gvgai.json with every clause that decided each entry: file, line, text, and what was read.

- **A1** holds iff the avatar present at level start is of a class that responds to at least two actions: every class except NullAvatar and MissileAvatar. Fails if no avatar is present.
- **A2** holds iff exactly one sprite's displacement is a function of the action, the avatar. It fails if the level does not place exactly one avatar sprite at start; if an interaction moves another sprite because the avatar moved into it (bounceForward, pullWithIt with the avatar as partner); and if the avatar's use action creates a sprite (ShootAvatar, FlakAvatar, ShootOnlyAvatar, AimedAvatar, OngoingShootAvatar) **[decided 24 Oct 2026]**. *Reason:* the predicate says only the avatar's displacement depends on the action; a shot is a sprite created by the action, another object responding to it. Study 1's census rule for A2 had no such clause (study 1, docs/PREREG.md v1, Section 5.3), and there it disagreed with the Procgen truth table on six games, five of them because the agent fires or throws and one (miner) because a push moves a boulder (study 1, docs/EXPLORATORY_V1_DIAGNOSIS.md, Tables 1 and 2). **[decided 25 Oct 2026]** A2 also fails wherever an action creates a sprite other than the avatar: an interaction that names an avatar type, in any position, and creates a sprite of a non-avatar type (a spawn effect, cloneSprite, or a transform into a non-avatar type); a transform of the avatar into another avatar type creates no other sprite. *Reason:* the predicate's purpose is that the action controls only the avatar's displacement, the reason already given for the use-action clause. In the deterministic suite it changes painter and zenpuzzle, both confirmatory (logs/truth_diff_q1.log); the decision was made before the tag with that count known (docs/truth/PREDICATE_QUESTIONS.md, Q1).
- **A3** holds iff each movement action, when not blocked, shifts the avatar by one constant vector whatever its position, facing and history: MovingAvatar, HorizontalAvatar, VerticalAvatar and FlakAvatar at speed 1; OrientedAvatar and ShootAvatar only with rotateInPlace=False. It fails for every other avatar class, for a speed other than 1, and where an interaction moves the avatar (teleportToExit, wrapAround, pullWithIt, bounceForward, windGust, slipForward, conveySprite, attractGaze).
- **A4** holds iff every move without effect is explained by a sprite that blocks the avatar (stepBack or undoAll with the avatar), every blocking type is of a class that never moves on its own (Immovable, Passive, Door, Resource, ResourcePack, SpawnPoint, SpawnPointMultiSprite, Portal, SpriteProducer), and blocking sprites are created, removed or moved only through the avatar's own actions: by interactions whose names include only agent-driven sprites, which are the avatar, the sprites its use action creates, and stationary-class sprites that only those push or pull. A sprite that kills on contact does not block. **[decided 25 Oct 2026; wording unchanged]** "With the avatar" is applied with the avatar named first. A blocker transformed into another blocking type in place counts as created and removed (rivers, a pilot game; no confirmatory verdict depends on it). A blocking relation that depends on the avatar's subtype holds A4, because the set of obstacles does not change during play. The other reading, that it fails, was considered and rejected before the tag: it would fail A4 on modality and tercio, 24 of 26 confirmatory games instead of 26 (logs/predicate_readings.log). It was rejected before the tag, with that count known, so that no predicate is adjusted after confirmatory data in view of what it does to the mechanics score's spread. The generator counts a name as standing for agent-driven sprites when every type it can place is agent-driven; this changes no verdict on any of the 550 units (logs/truth_diff_q4.log). The first clause is checked through every undoAll line that cancels the avatar's move, and a chain push or an edge wrap of a pushed box counts as the avatar's own (Section 1). The questions and reasons: docs/truth/PREDICATE_QUESTIONS.md, Q2 to Q4.
- **A5** holds iff the only winning condition is a SpriteCounter with limit 0 on one sprite type, the level holds exactly one sprite of that type, and every interaction that removes it has the avatar or an avatar subtype as partner; a precondition met elsewhere is recorded and keeps A5 holding.

**Mechanics score S_M** = the number of A1 to A5 that hold.

**Hand check.** Before the tag the author checks the generator by hand on 10 games drawn with seed 20261025 from the 30, every predicate, on level 0 and on one other level drawn with the same seed (scripts/draw_handcheck.py, logs/draw_handcheck.log; the sheet is docs/truth/HANDCHECK.md): clusters, colourescape, glow, labyrinthdual, lasers, painter, racebet, rivers, sokoban, wrapsokoban. Every disagreement is resolved from the GVGAI source, fixes the generator, and is listed in v1; the generator is rerun on all units and 5 fresh games are checked, which must agree on every predicate. **[decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 13]** The hand check is made by the author, with scripts/handcheck_app.py, which keeps his answers in docs/truth/handcheck_answers.json; scripts/handcheck_report.py writes the result to docs/truth/HANDCHECK_RESULT.md. The generator's automated checks do not replace it, and gate G4 (Section 8) stands as drafted.

## 6. Search quantities

Per (game, level), by the search job of docs/AUTOMATION.md, Sections 5 and 6:
- **State:** for every sprite, its type, cell, orientation and resources, and the avatar's health points; the game tick only where the description has a Timeout termination or a sprite with a cooldown, limit or total parameter. Identified by a 64-bit hash of a canonical serialization. GVGAI's tick limit is not part of the search.
- **Actions:** the adapter's action list. **Start:** the state after the level file is loaded. Win and loss states are counted and not expanded.
The lines below replace v0's definitions of R, L, b, EH, the primary score and the caps **[decided 14 Nov 2026, decisions 2, 4 and 6]**. v0 read: primary search score −log10 R, with R the number of distinct states reachable from the start; secondary L, b and EH; node cap 5,000,000 states expanded.
- **Ceiling.** The breadth-first search of a level stops when it has seen **C** states, or when its frontier is empty (complete), whichever comes first; the check falls between expansions.
- **C = [open; fixed before the tag by the rule below].** *Rule (decision 4):* the author fixes C when logs/stage_e_reading.log is committed, with its effect on the development counts known: for each candidate ceiling, the units determined, the share at or beyond the ceiling, and the AUROC of L_C and H_C overall, per family and on same-mechanics pairs (Section 3 of that log), and the power on the confirmatory structure (Section 4). C is at most 2,000,000, the seen cap of stage E, so that its effect on the development units can be read exactly. The value is entered on this line with the date it was fixed and the statement that it was fixed before any confirmatory game was played, and with the counts of the pilot search (Section 8) at that C if they are committed by then. The entry is part of v1 at the tag.
- **L_C** = the fewest actions from the start to a win state, if a win state is among the states seen when the search stops; otherwise "beyond C", ranked longest and tied with every other level beyond C. A level whose search completed without a win is beyond C.
- **H_C** = −log10 min(R, C), R the number of distinct states reachable from the start; a level that reached the ceiling sits at −log10 C, tied with every other such level.
- **b** = over the states expanded, the mean number of distinct successors other than the state itself.
- **Primary search score** = −L_C. Secondary: H_C and b. *Reason:* over pairs of development games with the same mechanics score, L ranked completion at 0.8277 for N against 0.6321 for the censored state count, and the test of Section 7.2 has power 0.7499 for N with L against 0.3140 with the state count at C = 1,000,000 (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Sections 6 and 7). L was already a secondary measure of v0.
- **The effective-horizon bound [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 6]** is dropped from every registered verdict and every registered secondary analysis. It is not deleted: its definition stays in docs/DESIGN.md, Section 5, and the supplement of the report gives it as an exploratory quantity, with its development AUROC, 0.5164 for N, 0.5155 for R and 0.6631 for I1 on the 893 units where it exists, and the share of development units on which it could not be computed, 667 of 1,560 (42.8%) (logs/dev_reading.log). *Reason:* the record should show that a measure with a theoretical basis carried nothing on this data.
- **Caps** (per level, summed over sessions): the ceiling C; time cap 14,400 s; memory ceiling 6 GiB resident per worker; frontier spill at 1,000,000 entries; checkpoint every 600 s or 250,000 expansions. The node cap of v0 is dropped, since a search sees at least as many states as it expands. The pilot search, which runs before C is fixed (Section 8), uses a seen cap of 2,000,000 and records the states seen and expanded at the first win, as stage E does, so that L_C and H_C can be read from it at every C up to 2,000,000.

## 7. Tests

### 7.1 Outcome
Per (agent, unit): 20,000 actions per seed, seeds 1000 to 1009. Y = 1 if the agent completed the level in at least 5 of the 10 seeds.

### 7.2 AUROC [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 3]
AUROC_L = the AUROC of −L_C for Y, over the pairs of a completed and an uncompleted confirmatory level **that come from different games with the same mechanics score S_M**; the Mann–Whitney statistic with ties counted ½. Pairs from the same game, and pairs from games with different S_M, are excluded. *Reason:* comparing only levels whose games have the same mechanics score holds H1's score fixed by construction, so the test asks whether search ranks completion where the mechanics score cannot tell the levels apart. *Cost:* with S_M at 2, 3, 4 and 5 on 3, 13, 7 and 3 games (logs/sdd.log), only pairs inside those four groups of games enter; scripts/analyze_horizon.py reports the number of pairs kept and their share of all between-game pairs.

v0 read: AUROC_H of −log10 R and AUROC_M of S_M, both over all between-game pairs **[changed 25 Oct 2026]**. Those two AUROCs, still over all between-game pairs, are now secondary (Section 7.7). The reason for excluding within-game pairs stands: the mechanics score is constant inside a game, so a within-game pair is scored one half for it by construction while a search score can order it. *Cost of that exclusion:* at most 6 within-game pairs per game, at most 156 over the 26 games: at most 10.3% of all pairs at an edge of the void band, 3.7% at a completed share of one half (logs/design_budget.log).

### 7.3 The interval [decided 14 Nov 2026]
The interval of AUROC_L is the percentile interval of 10,000 game-clustered bootstrap resamples: the 26 confirmatory games drawn with replacement, each with all its levels and its S_M. Pairs between two draws of the same game are within-game and excluded. Two-sided at 98.33% (Section 7.5). Seed 20261024 in 8 chunks (default_rng([20261024, k])) on a worker pool. Resamples with no eligible pair are skipped and counted. v0 put this interval on Δ = AUROC_H − AUROC_M; Δ keeps the same bootstrap, the same resample for both AUROCs, as a secondary estimate (Section 7.7).

### 7.4 Censoring at the ceiling [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 5; replaces the capped-game rule decided 24 Oct 2026]
1. A level whose search stops at the ceiling enters L_C and H_C as defined in Section 6: censored, not excluded.
2. A level whose search stops by the time cap or the memory ceiling before it has seen C states and without completing is excluded from the tests of L_C, H_C and b, unless a win state was seen, in which case it enters L_C with that length. It is listed with its partial counts.
3. A search quantity is void if more than 10% of the confirmatory levels are excluded under rule 2, that is, cannot reach C or complete. The share at the ceiling is reported, and is not a reason to void.
4. Every analysis reports the number and share of levels complete, at the ceiling, beyond C for L_C, and excluded.

*Reason:* on development data every level capped by nodes had seen more states than any complete level has, every level capped by time had seen at least 3,091,281 states, and the AUROC of the censored state count was 0.7115 to 0.7158 for N at every ceiling from 10,000 to 5,000,000 states (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 3). The 25% rule of v0 would have voided a score that kept its AUROC: on the development data 487 of 1,560 units were capped (31.2%), so every search measure would have been void under it (logs/dev_reading.log). The effect of C on L_C and H_C on the development units is in logs/stage_e_reading.log, Section 3, at the C fixed (Section 6).

v0 read: (1) a unit stopped by the node cap enters R as the node cap, tied at the top, L as found or infinite, b over the states expanded, EH as not computed; (2) a unit stopped by time or memory before the node cap is excluded from R, b and EH, and from L unless a win was reached; (3) a search quantity is void if more than 25% of the confirmatory units are capped by any cap, or more than 10% are excluded under (2); (4) every analysis reports the capped counts per cap.

### 7.5 Verdict per evidence agent [decided 14 Nov 2026; multiplicity and floor decided 24 Oct 2026]
Multiplicity unchanged: the family is the three evidence agents N, R and I1, and each interval of AUROC_L is a two-sided 98.33% interval (Bonferroni, 0.05 / 3).
- **Search ranks completion among mechanics-matched levels:** the interval's lower end > 0.5 and AUROC_L ≥ 0.70.
- **Not shown:** otherwise.

**The floor of 0.70 stays [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 9].** For N, with L on same-mechanics pairs, 0.7615 of the simulated studies on the confirmatory structure have the point at or above 0.70 and the lower end of the 98.33% interval above 0.5 (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 6, "both at 98.33%"); the normal approximation for the interval alone gives 0.7499.

v0 read: *H2 over H1* if the lower end of Δ's interval > 0 and AUROC_H ≥ 0.70; *H1 over H2* if its upper end < 0 and AUROC_M ≥ 0.70; *neither* otherwise.

### 7.6 Void rules

- The study is **void** if fewer than 20 confirmatory games remain, or if two of the three evidence agents are void **[changed 25 Oct 2026]**. ### 7.8 The smallest detectable difference [decided 24 Oct 2026] and the power of the primary test [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 12]
d = **0.51**, the smallest detectable difference of the secondary Δ (logs/sdd.log, entered 14 November 2026). It is reported beside Δ; it no longer governs the outcome table **[decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 7]**.

Estimate 1, from the development data: 0.3493 for N, 0.3439 for R, 0.3208 for I1. Estimate 2, on the confirmatory structure **[changed 25 Oct 2026]**: 0.4775 for N, 0.4757 for R, 0.5059 for I1 (logs/sdd.log). The largest, 0.5059, rounded up to two decimals, gives d. The mechanics score's variation in the confirmatory suite, reported beside them: S_M is 2 on 3 games, 3 on 13, 4 on 7 and 5 on 3, and A1 and A4 hold on all 26 (logs/sdd.log) **[decided 25 Oct 2026: from 3, 11, 9 and 3 before the A2 decision]**.

Method: on stage D's development units, for each evidence agent, 4,000 simulated studies each draw 26 development games with replacement (each with all its units) and compute Δ over between-game pairs; SE is the standard deviation of Δ over them; the estimate is (z(1 − α/2) + z(0.80)) × SE with α = 0.05 / 3. Estimate 2 **[changed 25 Oct 2026]** repeats this on the confirmatory structure: 26 clusters of 5 units, each carrying the actual S_M of a confirmatory game drawn with replacement and the search scores and outcomes of 5 units of a development game; there the mechanics score has no relation to the outcome, so the estimate measures the spread of Δ under the suite's own S_M values and ties. d is the larger estimate, the largest over N, R and I1, rounded up to two decimals. It is the true difference at which the registered interval would exclude zero with probability 0.80, under a normal approximation. *Reason:* a report of Δ must state the size of difference it could rule out. v0 read: **"A Refute outcome reads 'no difference of at least d between the search score and the mechanics score'"**; Δ no longer decides the outcome, so a secondary Δ whose interval includes zero reads "no difference of at least d", not "the data could not tell". d was computed with −log10 R under the capped-game rule of v0, on the stage D rows; it is not recomputed with H_C. If d is so large that the study is uninformative, the author decides before the tag whether to proceed, widen the suite or change the claim, and records the decision in v1: **decided 14 November 2026, change the claim (decision 1, option E; Section 1).**

**The power of the primary test [decided 14 Nov 2026, decision 12]:** **[open; entered before the tag at the C fixed in Section 6]**, for N, R and I1, the probability that the lower end of the 98.33% interval of AUROC_L lies above 0.5, and the share of simulated studies that also have AUROC_L at or above 0.70, if the confirmatory AUROC is near the development one. Method: that of logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 6, as scripts/stage_e_reading.py, Section 4, applies it to stage E's rows at each candidate C: 4,000 simulated studies of 26 clusters of 5 units, each cluster taking 5 units of one development game and the S_M of one confirmatory game, each drawn with replacement; the normal approximation of scripts/sdd.py. *Reason:* the registration should say how likely the primary test was to find a difference of the size the development data show, as it says d for the contrast. On the stage D rows, before C is fixed, the value for N with L on same-mechanics pairs is 0.7499 (logs/stage_e_diagnosis.log, Section 6).

| gate | passes if |
|---|---|
| G1 | the adapter, JDK and JPype run in a Kaggle session from a clean start, and each of N, R, I1 and Rand plays 20,000 actions on 2 seeds of every pilot unit without an error |
| G2 | on every pilot unit, 1,000 random action sequences of length 200, each replayed twice from the start, give identical state hashes at every step |

**Stop rule.** If any gate fails, no confirmatory unit runs.
