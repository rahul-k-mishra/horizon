# Reading of the oracle test (EXPLORATORY)

Every paragraph of this file is exploratory. The numbers come from the author's run of scripts/oracle_v2.py on 24 October 2026; the console is logs/oracle_v2.log, and the tables are docs/oracle/oracle_v2.md and docs/oracle/oracle_v2.json. Nothing here is registered, nothing here is a claim, and no fix is proposed as a result. Where a verdict word appears (PASS, FAIL, neither, VOID), it is what study 1's V2 rule prints when applied to a score it was not registered for.

## 1. The run and its checks

*Exploratory.* The script read 10,500 row files and kept 10,080 confirmatory rows (1,610 per agent and condition on MiniGrid/BabyAI, 140 per agent on Procgen center_off), skipped 420 pilot rows, and found 0 rows with an error field and 0 rows short of their budget. Both study-1 analysis files it imported match their blob hashes registered at study 1's prereg-v1.1. Three checks passed on 20 of 20 lines: the census lines it recomputed are identical to study 1's docs/tables/v2.json, its own bootstrap loop reproduces study 1's intervals, and its logistic refit reproduces study 1's baseline. So the oracle columns below differ from study 1's V2 in one thing only, the mechanics vector (logs/oracle_v2.log, lines 3 to 20 and 118 to 120).

## 2. What the oracle shows, per suite and condition

*Exploratory.* **MiniGrid/BabyAI, absolute moves (macro).** With the truth table in place of the census, the AUROC of the oracle score S for first-level completion is 0.6967 for I1 on its primary line (83 games, interval 0.6356 to 0.7583), 0.5527 for R (112 games; void, as in study 1, because R completed 101 of 112) and 0.5617 for N (interval 0.4289 to 0.6667). With the excluded environments put back it is 0.6281 (I1, D9b included) and 0.6617, 0.6286 and 0.6269 for I1, N and R over all 161 games. Under macro the oracle is not better than study 1's failed census on any line: the census scored 0.7187, 0.6985 and 0.6613 on the same three primary lines, and 0.7517 for I1 over all games. Study 1's rule would print "neither" for I1 and FAIL for N on the primary lines; no oracle line reaches 0.80 (logs/oracle_v2.log, lines 18 to 52).

*Exploratory.* **MiniGrid/BabyAI, primitive actions (raw).** The oracle AUROC on the primary lines is 0.6690 for I1 (98 games, interval 0.6119 to 0.7244), 0.5594 for R (155 games, 0.4843 to 0.6203) and 0.5477 for N. Here the oracle is above the census on every primary line (census 0.5370, 0.5224 and 0.5255), but study 1's rule would still print FAIL for R and N. Under raw, A3 fails on every game, so the conjunctive prediction P is false everywhere and its balanced accuracy is 0.5000 by construction (lines 53 to 87; docs/oracle/oracle_v2.md, Table 5).

*Exploratory.* **Procgen, whole-level view (center_off).** Over all 14 games the oracle AUROC is 0.7917 for I1 (interval 0.5222 to 1.0000) and 0.7556 for R and for N (0.5000 to 0.9583), against the census's 0.6458 and 0.7222. On the 9 in-range games it is 0.8250 for I1 (0.4375 to 1.0000) and 0.7250 for R and N (0.3750 to 1.0000), against the census's 0.8750 and 0.8000. P holds on 1 of the 14 games, so its balanced accuracy is between 0.5556 and 0.6250. R and N have the same outcome on every Procgen game, so their lines are identical (lines 88 to 117).

*Exploratory.* **In one sentence per suite:** on MiniGrid/BabyAI the exact mechanics rank the games by first-level completion at AUROC 0.55 to 0.70, no better than chance on some lines and never better than the census under macro; on Procgen they rank them at 0.73 to 0.83, with intervals too wide to separate that from chance on most lines.

## 3. The leave-one-out fits of the oracle are below chance on every MiniGrid line

*Exploratory.* The logistic model on the oracle vector (A1 to A5 as 0 and 1) has an in-sample AUROC of 0.5498 to 0.7157 on the 14 MiniGrid/BabyAI lines, but its leave-one-game-out AUROC is below 0.5 on all 14: from 0.4382 (I1, macro primary) down to 0.1809 (N, raw primary). On Procgen it is 0.4167 to 0.5333 (logs/oracle_v2.log, "models" lines; docs/oracle/oracle_v2.md, Table 4).

*Exploratory.* What that means follows from the shape of the vector, and is a reading, not a separate measurement. On MiniGrid/BabyAI the oracle vector takes three patterns only (Section 5), so the model is a lookup of three completion rates. Leaving one game out lowers its pattern's rate if the game completed and raises it if it did not, so within each pattern the left-out completing games are scored below the left-out non-completing ones. When the three rates differ by little, that reversal outweighs the differences between patterns and the AUROC falls below 0.5. Below-chance leave-one-out is therefore not evidence that mechanics predict the opposite of completion. It says that the in-sample gain of the oracle model is the gain of fitting three group rates, and that this gain does not carry to a game left out of the fit. The in-sample oracle numbers are not held-out estimates, and the MiniGrid oracle vector carries no out-of-sample information about completion that these fits can find.

## 4. What the search features show

*Exploratory.* **Before any agent runs: grid area, action count, colour count.** On MiniGrid/BabyAI, grid area alone ranks the games at 0.6817 to 0.8666 on the six primary lines (for example 0.8666 for I1 under macro, 0.7840 for R under macro, 0.6890 for R under raw). The paired difference area minus oracle is above zero with its interval clear of zero on five of the six (I1 and R under both conditions, and N under raw; for N under macro the interval is -0.0653 to 0.3842). When the excluded environments are put back, area falls to 0.4484 and 0.4578 for N and R under macro, below the oracle. Action count is near 0.5 on every MiniGrid line (0.3850 to 0.5278), and colour count is 0.4441 to 0.6390. The three together (study 1's baseline B) reach 0.5589 to 0.8734 in-sample and 0.4620 to 0.8422 leave-one-out on MiniGrid. On Procgen, grid area and action count are 0.5000 with intervals 0.5000 to 0.5000, because every game has the same 64 by 64 frame and the same six keys (study 1, docs/PREREG.md v1 Section 3.3 and v1.1 D1), so only colour count varies; B's leave-one-out AUROC there is 0.0000 to 0.2667 (docs/oracle/oracle_v2.md, Tables 2 to 4).

*Exploratory.* **Read out of N's runs: N's distinct states in level 0 and N's actions to its first level end.** These two are much stronger. N states ranks the games at 0.7511 to 0.9193 on the R and I1 lines of MiniGrid/BabyAI and at 0.8750 to 1.0000 on Procgen. N actions reaches 0.9880 to 0.9950 on the R lines of MiniGrid/BabyAI, 1.0000 on the R lines of Procgen, 0.8145 to 0.8974 on the I1 lines of MiniGrid/BabyAI, and 0.9167 and 1.0000 on the two I1 lines of Procgen. The paired difference N states minus oracle is above zero, with its interval clear of zero, on 9 of the 10 R and I1 lines of MiniGrid/BabyAI (the exception is R under macro primary, -0.1034 to 0.4725) and on R's line over all 14 Procgen games (logs/oracle_v2.log, "search" lines; docs/oracle/oracle_v2.md, Tables 2 and 3).

*Exploratory.* These two features cannot be read as the size of the level's search. On N's own lines they are circular: N's outcome and N's actions to its first level end describe the same runs, and N actions is 1.0000 on every N line. On the R and I1 lines they are post hoc. They exist only after an agent has played the game for 20,000 actions, they record how hard N found the level, and a high AUROC says that R's and I1's outcomes agree with N's. A seed where N never ended level 1 enters N actions as the budget itself, so part of the separation may be the censoring flag and not the count; scripts/oracle_sat.py was written to show how much (not yet run). N's state count also depends on N's assumptions, since N stalls where they fail, so it mixes search size with mechanics and cannot by itself tell H1 from H2.

## 5. Limits

*Exploratory.* **The MiniGrid/BabyAI truth vector varies only in A4 and A5.** A1 and A2 hold on all 161 games, A3 holds on all under macro and fails on all under raw, so S takes two values in each condition: 4 or 5 under macro (130 and 31 games) and 3 or 4 under raw (130 and 31). P holds on 31 games under macro and on none under raw (docs/oracle/oracle_v2.md, Table 5; study 1, docs/GROUND_TRUTH_minigrid.md, "Limits and findings", item 1). H1 was therefore tested on MiniGrid/BabyAI through one contrast, place goal and fixed obstacles against the rest, and not through the full vector.

*Exploratory.* **Procgen has 9 to 14 games.** Every Procgen oracle interval reaches 1.0000 at the top or sits at 0.5000 at the bottom, and so do most search-feature intervals. S takes four values over 14 games (2, 3, 4 and 5 on 5, 6, 2 and 1 games). Procgen is the only suite here where the mechanics vary much, and it is too small to separate a mechanics effect from chance.

*Exploratory.* **Nothing here is registered or controlled for multiplicity.** There are 20 lines, 5 features, 4 models and paired differences, read after study 1's outcome was known, by the author of the hypotheses. The lines are not independent: the same games appear in several subsets, and R and N have identical Procgen outcomes. No interval here is adjusted, and none of these numbers can serve as a test of H1 or H2.

## 6. Which hypothesis the registered study must now be built around

*Exploratory; a reading of these numbers, not a plan.* Exact mechanics, the best case for H1, ranked first-level completion on MiniGrid/BabyAI at 0.55 to 0.70 and gave nothing out of sample. The one measure of size known before any agent runs, grid area, ranked the same games better on five of the six primary lines. Measures of how much searching the level took ranked them better still, though only after the fact. So the reading is that the registered study must be built around H2, the size of the search the first level presents, measured before any agent runs, with H1, the match between mechanics and assumptions, as the rival it must beat. The same numbers also say that H1 has not had a fair test: on MiniGrid/BabyAI its vector barely varies, and on Procgen, where it does vary, it ranked the games at 0.73 to 0.83 on too few games to tell.
