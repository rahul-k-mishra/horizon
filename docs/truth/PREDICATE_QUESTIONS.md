# Four questions on the predicates, for the author's decision before the tag

Written 25 October 2026. Each question is a place where the wording of a registered predicate (docs/PREREG.md draft v0, Section 5; docs/DESIGN.md, Section 4) can be read two ways, or where the generator's verdict follows the wording but a reader of the game could still disagree with the answer key. No predicate has been changed and docs/truth/gvgai.json is unchanged by this file. Each question has to be decided by the author and the decision recorded before the tag prereg-v1.

How much each other reading would change is EXPLORATORY and comes from scripts/predicate_readings.py (logs/predicate_readings.log). The script recomputes one predicate on every game with one reading changed and lists every unit that changes. It first checks that its copy of the generator's A4 gives the generator's verdict on 550 of 550 units. Of the four games, painter and colourescape are confirmatory. rivers and labyrinthdual are pilot games (docs/PREREG.md, Section 4) and are left out of every confirmatory analysis. S_M on the 26 confirmatory games is now 2: 3 games, 3: 11, 4: 9, 5: 3.

## Q1. painter, A2: does a sprite created by walking count?

**What the description says.** third_party/gvgai/examples/gridphysics/painter.txt. The avatar is a MovingAvatar (line 15) and has no use action that creates anything. Walking onto ground spawns an invisible prepaint sprite (line 29, ground avatar > spawn stype=prepaint). Over the following steps that sprite turns the ground into paint (lines 30 to 32). Walking onto paint does the reverse through preground (lines 35 to 38). Nothing but the avatar ever changes place. What the action changes besides the avatar's place is which tiles are ground and which are paint.

**What the predicate says.** "Exactly one sprite's displacement is a function of the action: the avatar." It fails on three listed conditions: the level does not hold exactly one avatar, a push or pull moves a sprite the avatar walks into, or the avatar's use action creates a sprite (five shooter classes named). The reason given for the third condition (Decision 2) is wider than the condition: "a shot is a sprite created by the action, so another object responds to the action."

**Does the verdict follow?** The generator holds A2. That follows from the three listed conditions, since none of them applies to painter. The wording is ambiguous, because the listed condition and its stated reason point different ways. Under the condition as listed, only the use action of a shooter counts. Under the reason, any sprite the action creates counts, and walking creates one here. Read as "displacement", the head of the predicate holds: the prepaint sprite never moves.

**If read the other way** (A2 also fails where an interaction with the avatar as partner creates a sprite, by a spawn or a transform of a sprite other than the avatar): 45 units in 9 games change from holds to fails. 2 of them are confirmatory, painter and zenpuzzle. In zenpuzzle, walking turns ground into walked (line 24) and walking on walked spawns isincenter (line 25). A2 would hold on 7 of the 26 confirmatory games instead of 9, and S_M would become 2: 3, 3: 13, 4: 7, 5: 3.

**For the author to decide:** whether a sprite created by a movement action counts as another object responding to the action, as the reason for Decision 2 says, or whether only the listed use-action case counts.

## Q2. rivers, A4: does replacing one house with another count as changing the blockers?

**What the description says.** third_party/gvgai/examples/gridphysics/rivers.txt. Walls, rocks and houses block the avatar (line 32). Water spreads by itself from the waterOn sprites the level places (lines 17 to 19, a Spreader and a SpawnPoint). It dies on ground (line 34) and flows on only through holes, which the avatar digs with its shovel (line 40, ground shovel > transformTo stype=hole; line 36). When water reaches a dry house, the house turns into a wet house in the same cell (line 39, dryHouse water > transformTo stype=waterHouse). Both kinds of house block, so the cells that block the avatar are the same before and after.

**What the predicate says.** Blocking sprites must be "created, removed or moved only through the avatar's own actions", where an interaction is the avatar's own when the sprites it names are agent-driven: the avatar, the sprites its use action creates, and stationary sprites that only those push or pull. DESIGN, Section 4, adds: "A blocker ... that a producer, a spawn effect or a transform effect can create other than through the avatar, fails A4." The heading there is "a fixed set of blockers."

**Does the verdict follow?** The generator fails A4: line 39 removes a dryHouse and creates a waterHouse, and its partner, water, is not agent-driven. That follows from the wording as written. Water is a Spreader, not one of the three agent-driven kinds, and DESIGN names a transform effect as a cause of failure. A reader could still disagree in two ways. First, the water reaches a house only through holes the avatar dug, so the change does happen "through the avatar's own actions" in a looser sense. Second, the set of blocked cells never changes, so the assumption the heading names, a fixed set of blockers, is not broken. The wording speaks of sprites, not cells, and it does not settle whether a blocker turning into another blocker in place counts.

**If read the other way** (a transform of a blocker into another blocking type in the same cell is not counted as creating or removing a blocker): 10 units in 2 games change from fails to holds, rivers (pilot) and waitforbreakfast (not deterministic). No confirmatory unit changes: A4 holds on 26 of 26 confirmatory games either way, and S_M stays the same.

**For the author to decide:** whether A4 is about blocking sprites, as worded, or about blocked cells, as the heading suggests. Also whether a chain that starts with the avatar's own action and goes on through a sprite that moves by itself counts as the avatar's own.

## Q3. labyrinthdual, A4: does a set of blockers that changes with the avatar's coat count as fixed?

**What the description says.** third_party/gvgai/examples/gridphysics/labyrinthdual.txt. Every wall is Immovable (lines 7 to 10), and no wall is ever created, removed or moved. Which walls block depends on the avatar's subtype: normal walls block every avatar (line 22), avatarNormal is blocked by every wall (line 23), avatarBlue by red walls (line 24) and avatarRed by blue walls (line 25). Picking up a coat turns the avatar into avatarRed or avatarBlue (lines 28 and 31). So after a coat, walls that blocked the avatar no longer do, and the walls themselves are unchanged.

**What the predicate says.** The three conditions are on the blocking sprites: their class never moves on its own, and they are created, removed or moved only through the avatar's own actions. Nothing in the wording speaks of which avatar subtype a sprite blocks. The heading in DESIGN is "a fixed set of blockers."

**Does the verdict follow?** The generator holds A4. That follows from the wording: the walls are Immovable and are never changed. Even the change of subtype is the avatar's own act. The wording is silent on the case and does not make it ambiguous. The question is whether the wording says what the assumption means. To an agent that learns from frames which cells it cannot enter, the blocked cells change when the coat is put on.

**If read the other way** (A4 also fails where the blocking types differ between the avatar's subtypes): 65 units in 13 games change from holds to fails. 2 of them are confirmatory, modality and tercio, and 2 are pilot games, bait and labyrinthdual. A4 would hold on 24 of the 26 confirmatory games instead of 26, and S_M would become 2: 5, 3: 9, 4: 9, 5: 3. This also bears on the registered limit in PREREG Section 1, which rests on A1 and A4 holding on all 26 confirmatory games (logs/sdd.log). Under this reading that would no longer be true of A4.

**For the author to decide:** whether a blocking relation that depends on the avatar's own subtype, changed only by the avatar's own act, is a fixed set of blockers.

## Q4. colourescape, A4: does undoAll block the avatar when the avatar is not named first?

**What the description says.** third_party/gvgai/examples/gridphysics/colourescape.txt. Walls block the avatar (line 45). Each coloured box is pushed by the avatar of its colour (lines 47, 50, 53, 56). An avatar of another colour walking into it triggers undoAll with the box named first and the avatar second (lines 48, 51, 54, 57). GVGAI's undoAll puts every sprite back to its last position, whichever sprite comes first in the line (third_party/gvgai/src/ontology/effects/unary/UndoAll.java, lines 40 to 57). So the avatar's move has no effect: the box blocks it. A box pushed into a wall, a switch, an exit or another box also triggers undoAll (line 59). The avatar is not named in that line, but its move is undone as well. A box pushed onto a hole is removed (line 62).

**What the predicate says.** "Every move the avatar makes without effect is explained by a sprite that blocks it (stepBack or undoAll with the avatar)". The wording says "with the avatar", not "with the avatar first". The generator's documentation says "with the avatar first" (scripts/truth_vgdl.py, header, A4).

**Does the verdict follow?** The generator holds A4, counting only the walls as blockers, because it takes the avatar-first reading. The wording is ambiguous: "with the avatar" covers lines 48 to 57 as well, and under that reading the four boxes are blockers too. A third point is not reached by either reading. The generator never checks the clause "every move without effect is explained by a sprite that blocks it", so a move undone by line 59 passes unexamined. The same holds wherever a pushed box is stopped by undoAll in a line that does not name the avatar. The script does not count this case.

**If read the other way** (undoAll blocks the avatar wherever the avatar is named in the line): the boxes become blockers. They are Passive, moved only by avatars, and removed by a hole only after an avatar pushed them there. That fits the wording's own example of an agent-driven sprite (the doorkoban box). The generator as written would still fail A4 on colourescape, on 5 units, for a reason in its code rather than in the game. Line 62 names the parent type boxes, which is never placed in a level and has no moves of its own, so the generator does not count boxes as agent-driven. If a name is counted as agent-driven when all of its placeable types are (Q4b in the log), colourescape holds A4 under this reading too, and no unit changes. That change to the count alters no verdict under the present reading on any of the 550 units (logs/predicate_readings.log, self-check). With the fault left in, A4 would hold on 25 of the 26 confirmatory games and S_M would become 2: 3, 3: 12, 4: 8, 5: 3. With it fixed, nothing changes.

**For the author to decide:** whether undoAll blocks the avatar when the avatar is named in any position. Then whether the clause "every move without effect is explained by a blocker" is meant as a condition the generator must check (it does not now) or as a description of what the blocking lines do. If the first reading is adopted, the leaf-type count of Q4b goes into the generator with it.

## What deciding means here

A decision on any question goes into docs/DESIGN.md and docs/PREREG.md by a dated change. If it changes the generator, the generator is rerun on all units and the change is listed in v1, as the hand-check rule requires (docs/PREREG.md, Section 5). Until then the hand-check rows for these four cells show the generator's present verdict, and an answer of disagree or unsure there should note which reading it takes.
